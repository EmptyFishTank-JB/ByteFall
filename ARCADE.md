# THE ARCADE

Seven games on ByteFall's bits, the base games only (no daily game), out of ByteFall and on a page of their
own, `arcade.html` (on the site: the landing page's THE ARCADE), on their way to an app of their own
(the next one for Google Play, after refinement). They were ByteFall's DAILY ARCADE card and its ARCADE
menu; they left it so ByteFall keeps to its own game.

- **The page**: `arcade.html`, the card on its own, as tall as the screen and as wide as ByteFall's game
  card at most. It opens on the ARCADE MENU: every game as a card in a row that slides sideways (FREE
  PLAY). BACK in a game goes back to the menu; in the menu, to the landing page. `?game=trace` puts that
  game in the middle of the menu.
- **No daily game**: there was one a day (TODAY, Monday CODE BREAKER to Sunday ROUTING, the same for
  everyone, with SHARE); it's gone, along with its SHARE and the cards' DAILY ON ... line. Every game
  is FREE PLAY only. (Days already played left their saves, `bytefall-arcade-<game>-<date>`, unread.)
- **No KEYS, no streak, no achievements** for now: those were ByteFall's (`js/progress.js`). Free play
  keeps a best for each game (`bytefall-arcade-free-stats`). Its own rewards are for the app to come.
- **The files**: `arcade.html`; `js/arcade-shell.js` (what it used of ByteFall's script.js: storage,
  the seeded numbers, the bits as the theme draws them, SPECTRUM's spin), `js/arcade.js`
  (the frame: the menu, the line, the clock, saving, NEW GAME, the previews) and a file per game,
  `css/arcade.css`; and ByteFall's own `css/style.css` (the themes, the bits), `js/sfx.js`, `js/fx.js`,
  `js/grid-bg.js` and `js/fonts.js`, shared, so the looks and sounds are ByteFall's. The theme, font and
  REDUCED EFFECTS are ByteFall's settings (on the web, the same browser's); the arcade has no settings
  of its own yet.

## The games

**CODE BREAKER** (`js/arcade-codebreaker.js`): a 4-bit code, each bit [1] to [7] (repeats allowed), to crack in 10 tries. The board has the 4 columns a code needs: the code at the top (? till the game's over), a dashed line, then the 10 tries, filling from the bottom up as bits stack, each row's number at its left and **its pips in a row at its right**: lit, a right bit in the right place; hollow (the accent color), a right bit in the wrong place; dark, no match (the lit ones first, then the hollow, so they say how many, not which), lighting one at a time after ENTER. Under the board, what the pips mean, then the keypad: the seven bits as the theme draws them, DELETE and ENTER (or the keys 1 to 7, Backspace and Enter). A code already tried isn't taken: the error buzz, ALREADY TRIED // TRY 02 on the line, and that try's row pulses red three times, what's keyed in left to change.

**TRACE** (`js/arcade-trace.js`, Minesweeper's rules): a 9x12 board of encrypted blocks (the layers' own stripes), 16 INFECTED bits hidden under them, and a small patch open to start from (a block with nothing round it and what it opens: the smallest such patch on the board, 4 to 20 blocks; a board without one is drawn again from the same seed), so nobody's first tap is a guess. Tap a block to decrypt it: under it a bit counting the infected bits touching it (the 8 round it), or nothing, and then the blocks round it open by themselves, bursting as bits decrypt. Hold a block (or switch TAP: OPEN to TAP: FLAG; a right click too) to flag it, a crosshair that keeps it from opening; tap a bit with as many flags round it as its number to open the rest round it. An infected block opened ends it (INFECTED // TRACE FAILED: every infected one shows, the one hit in red, wrong flags crossed); every safe block open and the board's clean (the infected caught, in the bits' color). The clock runs from the first tap; the line shows it and the infected left (16 less the flags).

**CHECKSUM** (`js/arcade-checksum.js`, Sudoku's rules): the 9x9 grid in nine 3x3 boxes, about 30 bits given (made from its seed: a full grid, then bits taken out in pairs opposite each other through the middle as long as it keeps one answer only). Fill it so every row, column and box holds [1] to [9] once. Tap a square, then a bit in the row under the board to write it in (the same bit again takes it out; a given bit stays); with NOTES on, the bits are pencil marks instead, the ones it could be, small in a 3x3 in the square; ERASE clears the square; a bit written in comes off the notes round it. The picked square is outlined, its row, column and box tinted (the same bit elsewhere isn't lit: that would make it too easy); a bit with all nine in has its button dimmed. Notes are as bright as the bits written in (the accent). Given bits are the board's own, yours are in the accent color. CONFIRM checks it and says so, a big word over the grid and on the line: INCOMPLETE (and how many squares are empty), CHECKSUM FAILED (full but wrong somewhere: it doesn't say where; counted) or CHECKSUM VALID (the game over, the squares flashing). Timed: the clock runs whenever the card's up, till it's valid. Keys: 1 to 9, 0 or Backspace to erase, N for NOTES, the arrows to move, Enter to CONFIRM.

**MERGE** (`js/arcade-merge.js`, 2048's rules): a 4x4 board; swipe (or the arrows, or WASD) and every bit slides that way as far as it goes, two the same that meet merging into the next (each bit merges once a move): two [1]s a [2] ... two [7]s a BYTE, two BYTEs a WORD, then a DWORD, a QWORD (2048's own tile) and on, the higher the brighter, a BYTE and up in the accent by name. Each merge scores the new bit's worth (a [2] 4, a BYTE 256, a QWORD 2,048); after every move a [1] drops in (one in ten a [2]) (drawn in order from the game's seed, wherever they land). Every 10th move an ENCRYPTED block drops in too (two at most): it slides with the rest but never merges, and a merge beside it cracks it. The bits slide square to square, a merged one pops, new ones appear once the slide's done. No move left: NO MOVES LEFT over the board, the score and the best bit made. Not timed.

**ROUTING** (`js/arcade-routing.js`, Flow's rules): a 7x7 grid with six to eight pairs of bits, [1] up, each pair in its own color (red, orange, yellow, green, cyan, blue, violet, pink; MONOCHROME: no colors, the pairs told apart by their numbers); route a line from each bit to its twin, square to square (no diagonals), never crossing, till every pair is linked and every square has a line through it. Drag from a bit (or from a line's end, to carry it on); back over the line takes it back; across another line cuts it there; another pair's bit stops it; reaching its twin links it (its bits glow). Each square drawn ticks, each tick higher than the one before however long the line gets (about a semitone a square at first, less as it goes, never none). CLEAR ALL starts over. **Every grid has one way through only, and it fills every square**, and a pair's bits are never side by side (corner to corner can be): made from its seed, one path through every square (a snake, shuffled by thousands of "backbites") cut into runs that never run alongside themselves (a run ends where the next square would touch it anywhere but its end, or at a random length), their ends the pairs; a solver (each line grown from one bit, the one with the fewest ways on first, given up on wherever an empty square has fewer than two ways in, a line can't reach its twin, or a patch can't be crossed by any line) counts the ways to route it, and the grid's drawn again from the same seed till there's exactly one (a few hundredths of a second). The clock runs from the first line drawn; the line shows it, the ROUTES drawn, the pairs linked and how much is filled (TIME 0:42 // ROUTES 9 // LINKED 4/8 // 76%; all linked with squares still empty: ALL LINKED // n SQUARES EMPTY). **ROUTES** counts how many times a line's been drawn: a new one (a drag from a bit, the line started over) counts; carrying on the line drawn last doesn't; carrying on any other line does (it's taken up again). It counts once a drag draws or takes back a square, so a tap alone isn't a route, even one that wipes a line by its bit.

**DEFRAG** (`js/arcade-defrag.js`, swap-and-match): a 7x7 board full of bits, [1] to [6], each number its own color (MONOCHROME: none, the numbers tell them apart; GLYPH: its shapes, colored). Swap two side by side (a swipe from one into the other, or a tap on each, the first outlined) and **4 or more of a number touching** (up, down, left, right: a line, an L, a T, a square) decrypt, bursting; the bits above fall, new ones drop in from above, and whatever the fall puts together decrypts too, each fall a CHAIN link (CHAIN x2 on the line). A swap that matches nothing slides there and back (NO MATCH, and it isn't counted). A bit dropping in never makes a group by itself (its number moved on till it doesn't, as when the board's dealt), so what chains is what falls into place: about one swap in six sets one off. A bit decrypted scores 5 x (its group's size - 2) (a group of 4: 10 a bit, of 6: 20), x its chain link. **ENCRYPTED** bits (6 dealt; the layers' stripes, their numbers dim) can't be swapped or matched till a match beside one cracks it (+30). **INFECTED** bits (2 dealt; ByteFall's red frame and virus) are bits as any other, but every 5 swaps one passes it to a bit beside it (THE INFECTION SPREADS), till a match on or beside one cleans it (+20); each left at the end costs 20. No swap left: the bits are dealt again, free (NO SWAP LEFT). Idle 6 seconds, a swap that works pulses. **30 swaps** for the best score; then DEFRAGMENTED over the board. Made from the game's seed: its board, and the bits dropping in (in order, wherever they land). Not timed.

**DECODE** (`js/arcade-decode.js`, Picross's rules): a grid with a picture hidden in it, one of 23: the four bots (BOT, GRIFTER, BUNKER and GLITCH, a face each) and the visitors (the GHOST, FRANKENSTEIN, the MUMMY, the CREATURE, NOSFERATU, the GREMLIN, the JACK O' LANTERN, the FOG WANDERER, a ZOMBIE, the WEREWOLF, the BUG, the TROJAN, RANSOMWARE, SPYWARE, the LOGIC BOMB, a TURKEY, a PENGUIN, a REINDEER, a SNOWMAN), 9x9 to 16x14 or 13x21, drawn from their own pixel art, their eyes and mouths (a belly, a carved face) left as holes. The numbers at each row's left and over each column are its runs of filled squares, in order (3 1: three filled, a gap, one); a line's numbers dim once it's as they say, and the row and column under a finger light up. Tap a square to fill it (again to empty it), hold it (or TAP: MARK; a right click) to mark it empty, an X; a drag does the same along the row or column it sets off in, to the squares as the first one was (filling empty ones, or emptying ones like it). A brighter line every 5 squares, for counting. Every row and column as its numbers say and it's DECODED: the picture comes in corner to corner in its own colors (MONOCHROME: grays), eyes and all, the numbers fading, its name under it (IT'S THE GHOST). **Every picture has one answer only, found a line at a time with no guessing**: a line solver (every way a line's runs can lie that fits what's known of it: a square filled in all of them is filled, empty in all is empty) works each one out (picked and set so it does: the PHAGE, the BIG SPIDER and the CROW didn't, and aren't in). A picture at random each game; the clock runs from the first square; the line shows it and the rows and columns done (TIME 1:23 // ROWS 5/16 // COLUMNS 3/12).

**FREE PLAY** (the menu's cards): a free game is a new one each time (its own random seed), as many as you like; the one under way is kept (BACK goes back to the menu; closing the app keeps it too); once it's over, NEW GAME and your best (CODE BREAKER the fewest tries, MERGE and DEFRAG the highest score, the others the fastest time; NEW BEST! when it is; `bytefall-arcade-free-stats`).

**THE PREVIEWS**: over each card's name its game plays itself on a small board, drawn as the game's own (each game's `demo`): CODE BREAKER a code broken try by try, each try one that fits every try before it, the bits keyed in and the pips lighting; TRACE a board cleared a block at a time, a flag now and then, the infected caught; MERGE 28 moves, the bits kept to a corner; CHECKSUM a grid filled square by square, the surest first, notes pencilled in now and then, then CHECKSUM VALID; DEFRAG ten swaps, each the one that decrypts the most, chains and all; DECODE a picture worked out by the line solver a line at a time, filled and marked, then its colors; ROUTING a grid routed line by line, square by square (made as a game's are, but not checked for one answer only). Each runs 8 to 15 seconds, holds the finished board a moment, fades and starts a new game. Only the cards on screen play (the one in the middle and the two peeking in at its sides): one slid away holds where it was till it's back, and none play with the menu shut or the app in the background. Each is a small board of squares changing a few times a second, made in a few milliseconds. REDUCED EFFECTS (or reduced motion): each shows a finished board, still.
